# Historical Research: The Partition of Bengal (1905) & Revolutionary Nationalism

## 1. Executive Summary

On **16 October 1905**, the British Indian administration under Viceroy **Lord Curzon** enacted the first Partition of Bengal. The massive Lieutenant-Governorship of Bengal—which encompassed over 78 million inhabitants across Bengal proper, Bihar, Orissa, and Chota Nagpur—was divided into two separate provincial administrations:

1. **Eastern Bengal and Assam**: Capital at **Dacca** (Dhaka), comprising East Bengal, North Bengal (except Darjeeling), Chittagong, and Assam.
2. **Bengal** (often called *West Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa*): Capital remaining at **Calcutta** (Kolkata), comprising West Bengal, Bihar, Chota Nagpur, and Orissa.

---

## 2. Official Justification vs Political Context

### Official Administrative Rationale
- **Administrative Overload**: The Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal was responsible for nearly 80 million people, an area larger than France, with poor communications in East Bengal deltaic districts.
- **Assam's Isolation**: Assam, created as a Chief Commissionership in 1874, lacked a seaport and direct rail access to a major commercial center. Attaching Chittagong port and East Bengal districts was presented as an economic boost for Assam.

### Political & Strategic Motives
- **Weakening Nationalist Unity**: Curzon and Home Secretary Sir Herbert Risley explicitly noted in internal minutes that "Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull several different ways."
- **Religious Division**: By creating a Muslim-majority province in the East (capital Dacca) and a Hindu-majority province in the West (where Bengalis were reduced to a linguistic minority behind Hindi and Oriya speakers in the new Bengal province), the British aimed to foster communal counterweights to the Indian National Congress.

## 3. Revolutionary Secret Societies & Armed Resistance (1905–1911)

The Partition of Bengal catalyzed the birth of armed revolutionary nationalism in India. Dissatisfied with moderate petitions, young nationalists founded underground secret societies (*Samitis*) dedicated to ending British colonial rule through direct action.

### 3.1. Anushilan Samiti (Calcutta & Dhaka)
- **Founding**: Initiated in Calcutta in 1902 by **Pramathanath Mitra (P. Mitra)**, **Satish Chandra Basu**, and **Aurobindo Ghose**, inspired by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's *Anandamath*.
- **Dhaka Anushilan Samiti**: Founded in November 1905 by **Pulin Behari Das** following a rally in Dhaka. It grew into the most formidable secret revolutionary network in East Bengal, establishing over 500 branches across Dacca, Mymensingh, Faridpur, and Backergunge.
- **Ideology & Physical Training**: Combined physical fitness (*Lathi-khela*, swordplay, gymnastics) with spiritual discipline based on the Bhagavad Gita and Bankim Chandra's patriotic hymns.

### 3.2. Jugantar Group & Underground Publications
- **Jugantar Party**: Formed in 1906 by **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (younger brother of Aurobindo Ghose), **Bhupendranath Datta** (brother of Swami Vivekananda), and **Abhinash Bhattacharya**.
- **Underground Literature**: Published the radical revolutionary weekly journal *Jugantar* (New Era) and circulated Sri Aurobindo's political pamphlet *Bhavani Mandir* (Temple of the Goddess), which called for physical self-sacrifice and armed liberation.

### 3.3. The Alipore Bomb Case (1908) & Maniktala Garden House
- **Bomb Factory**: Barindra Ghosh established an underground bomb factory and military training camp at his family garden house in **Maniktala**, Calcutta.
- **Hemchandra Kanungo (Das)**: Traveled to Paris in 1906 to learn chemical bomb manufacturing from European anarchists and Russian revolutionaries, bringing the technical secrets back to Maniktala.
- **Muzaffarpur Bomb Outrage (30 April 1908)**: Young revolutionaries **Khudiram Bose** (aged 18) and **Prafulla Chaki** were dispatched to assassinate Douglas Kingsford, the notoriously repressive Chief Presidency Magistrate of Calcutta who had been transferred to Muzaffarpur (Bihar). Prafulla Chaki martyred himself to avoid arrest, while Khudiram Bose was captured and hanged on 11 August 1908, becoming an immortal folk martyr across Bengal.
- **Alipore Bomb Trial (1908–1909)**: Police raided the Maniktala garden house, arresting Sri Aurobindo, Barindra Ghosh, Ullaskar Dutta, and 34 others. **Deshbandhu Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das)** brilliantly defended Sri Aurobindo, securing his acquittal, while Barindra Ghosh and Ullaskar Dutta were sentenced to transportation for life to the Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands.

### 3.4. Bagha Jatin & Transnational Networks
- **Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin)**: Assumed leadership of the Jugantar network, organizing daring assassinations of high-ranking colonial police officers and secret agents (including public prosecutor Ashutosh Biswas and DSP Shamsul Alam).
- **Indo-German Conspiracy (WWI)**: Laid the groundwork for acquiring German arms during World War I, culminating in the famous battle at Balasore (1915).

## 4. Popular Agitation: Swadeshi & Boycott

- **16 October 1905 as Mourning & Union**: Declared a day of mourning (*Arandhan* - no hearth fires lit). **Rabindranath Tagore** introduced the **Rakhi Bandhan** ceremony, where Hindus and Muslims tied yellow threads on each other's wrists as a symbol of undivided brotherhood.
- **Swadeshi & Boycott**: Mass boycott of imported British goods (Manchester cloth, Liverpool salt) and promotion of indigenous industries (Bengal Chemical, Swadeshi stores, handloom weaving).

---

## 5. Annulment and 1911–1912 Reorganisation

Faced with persistent agitation and revolutionary violence, King George V announced the **annulment of the 1905 Partition** at the Delhi Durbar on **12 December 1911**:

1. **Re-unification of Bengal**: All Bengali-speaking divisions (Presidency, Burdwan, Rajshahi, Dacca, Chittagong) were reunited under a full **Governor in Council** for Bengal (Bengal Presidency).
2. **Creation of Bihar and Orissa**: Bihar, Chota Nagpur, and Orissa were carved out into a separate Province of Bihar and Orissa (capital Patna).
3. **Assam Restored**: Assam was separated back into a Chief Commissionership.
4. **Capital Shift to Delhi**: The capital of British India was officially moved from Calcutta to New Delhi, ending Calcutta's 140-year role as the imperial capital.
