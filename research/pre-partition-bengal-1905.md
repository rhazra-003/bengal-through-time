# Historical Research: Pre-Partition Bengal (c. 1905)

## 1. Executive Summary & Administrative Identity

This document establishes the historical geography, administrative hierarchy, and territorial boundaries of the **Province of Bengal** (officially the *Lieutenant-Governorship of Bengal*, often colloquially referred to as the *Bengal Presidency*) as it existed immediately prior to the Partition of Bengal taking effect on **16 October 1905**.

### Key Statistical Profile (1901 Census Baseline)
- **Official Name**: The Lieutenant-Governorship of Bengal
- **Headquarters / Winter Capital**: Calcutta (modern Kolkata)
- **Summer Capital**: Darjeeling
- **Head of Administration**: Lieutenant-Governor (Sir Andrew Fraser, 1903–1908)
- **Total Area**: ~189,837 sq miles (British territory) + ~36,500 sq miles (Feudatory Native States)
- **Total Population**: 74,744,866 (British territory) + ~3,748,544 (Native States) = ~78.49 Million (1901 Census)
- **Administrative Divisions**: 9 Divisions comprising 47 British Districts, plus attached Princely States.

---

## 2. Territorial Composition Immediately Before October 1905

Prior to 16 October 1905, the Lieutenant-Governorship of Bengal was a vast composite province comprising four distinct historical/geographical regions:

1. **Bengal Proper** (Deltaic East Bengal, North Bengal, West Bengal, Central Bengal)
2. **Bihar** (Patna, Tirhut, Bhagalpur divisions)
3. **Chota Nagpur** (South Bihar plateau / Jharkhand region)
4. **Orissa** (Coastal districts and Orissa Tributary Mahals)

## 3. Detailed Division and District Hierarchy

### 3.1. Presidency Division
- **Headquarters**: Calcutta
- **Districts (6)**:
  1. **Calcutta**: Provincial capital, premier port, judicial & administrative seat.
  2. **24-Parganas**: Headquarters at Alipore. Included suburban Calcutta, Sundarbans mangrove tracts, Diamond Harbour.
  3. **Nadia**: Headquarters at Krishnagar. Included Navadvip (classical seat of Sanskrit learning and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu), Santipur, Kushtia.
  4. **Murshidabad**: Headquarters at Berhampore. Historical seat of the Nawabs of Bengal (Murshidabad city, Jiaganj, Jangipur, Kandi).
  5. **Jessore**: Headquarters at Jessore. Included Jhenaidah, Magura, Narail.
  6. **Khulna**: Headquarters at Khulna. Formed in 1882 from Jessore & 24-Parganas portions. Included Satkhira, Bagerhat (Mosque City of Khan Jahan Ali).

### 3.2. Burdwan Division
- **Headquarters**: Burdwan (Bardhaman)
- **Districts (6)**:
  1. **Burdwan**: Headquarters at Burdwan. Included coal mining hub of Asansol, Raniganj, Kalna, Katwa.
  2. **Birbhum**: Headquarters at Suri. Included Bolpur (Santiniketan), Rampurhat.
  3. **Bankura**: Headquarters at Bankura. Historical Bishnupur kingdom (famous for terracotta temples), Ranibandh.
  4. **Midnapore**: Headquarters at Midnapore. Largest district by area in Bengal Proper. Included Tamluk (ancient Tamralipta port), Contai, Ghatal, Jhargram.
  5. **Hooghly**: Headquarters at Hooghly (with Chinsura). Included Serampore (Danish settlement until 1845), Chandernagore (French enclave), Arambagh.
  6. **Howrah**: Headquarters at Howrah. Heavy industrial and railway terminus hub created as separate district in 1843/1904.

### 3.3. Rajshahi Division
- **Headquarters**: Jalpaiguri
- **Districts (8)**:
  1. **Rajshahi**: Headquarters at Rampur Boalia (Rajshahi town). Included Natore (seat of Rani Bhabani), Naogaon.
  2. **Dinajpur**: Headquarters at Dinajpur. Included Thakurgaon, Balurghat.
  3. **Jalpaiguri**: Headquarters at Jalpaiguri. Created in 1869 following Western Duars annexation.
  4. **Darjeeling**: Headquarters at Darjeeling. Hill station, tea plantations, annexed 1835/1850. Included Kalimpong, Kurseong, Siliguri.
  5. **Rangpur**: Headquarters at Rangpur. Included Kurigram, Gaibandha, Nilphamari.
  6. **Bogra**: Headquarters at Bogra. Included ancient ruins of Mahasthangarh (Pundranagara).
  7. **Pabna**: Headquarters at Pabna. Included Sirajganj (major jute trading centre on the Jamuna/Brahmaputra).
  8. **Malda**: Headquarters at English Bazar. Included ruins of historical capitals Gour (Gaur) and Pandua.

### 3.4. Dacca (Dhaka) Division
- **Headquarters**: Dacca
- **Districts (4)**:
  1. **Dacca**: Headquarters at Dacca. Historical Mughal capital of Bengal (Jahangirnagar), famous for muslin textiles. Included Narayanganj port, Munshiganj (Bikrampur).
  2. **Mymensingh**: Headquarters at Mymensingh (Nasirabad). Most populous district in Bengal Presidency (>4.3 million in 1901). Included Jamalpur, Tangail, Kishoreganj, Netrakona.
  3. **Faridpur**: Headquarters at Faridpur. Included Madaripur, Gopalganj, Goalundo Ghat (major steamship/rail hub).
  4. **Backergunge**: Headquarters at Barisal. Major rice bowl of East Bengal, Sundarbans tract, Pirojpur, Bhola, Patuakhali.

### 3.5. Chittagong Division
- **Headquarters**: Chittagong
- **Districts (4)**:
  1. **Chittagong**: Major seaport and naval gateway (Porto Grande). Included Cox's Bazar.
  2. **Chittagong Hill Tracts**: Headquarters at Rangamati. Special frontier administrative tract inhabited by Chakma, Marma, and Tripura indigenous peoples.
  3. **Noakhali**: Headquarters at Noakhali (Sudharam). Included Feni, Lakshmipur, Sandwip island.
  4. **Tippera**: Headquarters at Comilla. Included Brahmanbaria, Chandpur port on the Meghna.

### 3.6. Patna Division (Bihar)
- **Headquarters**: Patna
- **Districts (7)**:
  1. **Patna**: Ancient Pataliputra, major agricultural trading capital on the Ganges. Headquarters at Patna.
  2. **Gaya**: Headquarters at Gaya. Major pilgrimage centre (Bodh Gaya, Vishnupad). Included Jehanabad, Nawada.
  3. **Shahabad**: Headquarters at Arrah. Included Buxar (Battle of Buxar 1764), Sasaram (Sher Shah Suri Tomb), Rohtasgarh fort.
  4. **Saran**: Headquarters at Chapra. Included Siwan, Gopalganj.
  5. **Champaran**: Headquarters at Motihari. Included Bettiah, Bettiah Raj estate.
  6. **Muzaffarpur**: Headquarters at Muzaffarpur. Included Hajipur, Sitamarhi.
  7. **Darbhanga**: Headquarters at Darbhanga. Seat of the Raj Darbhanga estate. Included Madhubani, Samastipur.

### 3.7. Bhagalpur Division (Bihar)
- **Headquarters**: Bhagalpur
- **Districts (4)**:
  1. **Bhagalpur**: Headquarters at Bhagalpur. Ancient Anga kingdom region on the Ganges. Included Banka.
  2. **Monghyr (Munger)**: Headquarters at Monghyr. Capital of Nawab Mir Qasim, famous for fort and gun manufacturing. Included Jamui, Begusarai.
  3. **Santal Parganas**: Headquarters at Dumka. Special Non-Regulation district created after the Santhal Rebellion of 1855. Included Deoghar, Rajmahal, Sahibganj, Pakur.
  4. **Purnea**: Headquarters at Purnea. Bordering Nepal and North Bengal. Included Katihar, Kishanganj.

### 3.8. Orissa Division
- **Headquarters**: Cuttack
- **Districts (5)**:
  1. **Cuttack**: Headquarters at Cuttack. Historic capital of Orissa on the Mahanadi delta. Included Jajpur, Kendrapara.
  2. **Puri**: Headquarters at Puri. Home of the Jagannath Temple and Konark Sun Temple. Included Khurda.
  3. **Balasore**: Headquarters at Balasore. Coastal port town with early English, French, and Dutch factories. Included Bhadrak.
  4. **Angul**: Headquarters at Angul. Direct British administered tract; included the Kandhmals tract.
  5. **Sambalpur**: Headquarters at Sambalpur. Transferred from the Central Provinces to Orissa Division in October 1905 during the boundary adjustments.

### 3.9. Chota Nagpur Division (Jharkhand)
- **Headquarters**: Ranchi
- **Districts (5)**:
  1. **Ranchi**: Headquarters at Ranchi (Lohardaga renamed Ranchi in 1899). Plateau region, Mundari indigenous belt, Khunti (Birsa Munda uprising 1899–1900).
  2. **Hazaribagh**: Headquarters at Hazaribagh. Plateau district including Parasnath hill (sacred Jain pilgrimage peak), Giridih.
  3. **Palamau**: Headquarters at Daltonganj. Created in 1892 from Lohardaga. Included Garhwa.
  4. **Manbhum**: Headquarters at Purulia. Major coalfields (Jharia), Dhanbad sub-division.
  5. **Singbhum**: Headquarters at Chaibasa. Iron ore region, Kolhan tract, Jamshedpur (developed slightly later by Tatas).

## 4. Feudatory / Princely States under Bengal Supervision (1905)

1. **Cooch Behar State**: Maharaja Nripendra Narayan Bhup Bahadur. Enclave state enclosed by Dinajpur, Rangpur, Jalpaiguri, and Goalpara.
2. **Hill Tippera (Tripura State)**: Maharaja Radha Kishore Manikya Bahadur. Capital at Agartala. Independent native state in ceremonial political alliance with Bengal Government.
3. **Orissa Tributary States (24 states)**: Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar, Dhenkanal, Gangpur, Bonai, Bamra, Rairakhol, Sonpur, Athgarh, Baramba, Daspalla, Hindol, Khandpara, Narsinghpur, Nayagarh, Nilgiri, Pal Lahara, Ranpur, Talcher, Tigiria, Baud, Athmallik.
4. **Chota Nagpur Tributary States**: Surguja, Jashpur, Udaipur, Korea, Chang Bhakar (transferred to Central Provinces in October 1905 exchange).

## 6. Cartographic & GeoJSON Reconstruction Standards

- **Projection**: WGS 84 (EPSG:4326) Decimal Degrees.
- **Bounding Box**: Longitude `81.0° E` to `92.5° E`, Latitude `19.0° N` to `27.5° N`.
- **Topological Integrity**: Neighboring district boundaries share exact vertex nodes to prevent overlaps or gaps.
- **Metadata Fields**: Every GeoJSON Feature includes `id`, `name`, `historicalName`, `division`, `divisionId`, `type`, `year`, `confidence`, `sources`.
